# ASMR squishy obby

A browser obstacle course that never ends and keeps climbing, played online alongside everyone else on the same course. You hop up across squishies over a sea of bubbling lava: dumplings in bamboo steamers, holey cheese blocks, sugar-dusted jelly cubes, sticks of butter, carrots, and bumpy cucumbers. Each one wears a dumpling face. Some pads are towers of wooden ABC toy blocks instead: solid, with no squish and a quicker knock when you land. Landings squash and jiggle the squishy, sink you into it, and play a cereal-bowl crunch that gets louder on a harder hit. The colliders stay a fixed shape, so every avatar jumps the same. Snowy mountains and a rainbow sit on the horizon.

## Run locally

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open http://localhost:41723

The dev server listens on `0.0.0.0` port **41723**.

## Play

- Desktop: WASD or the arrow keys to move, Space to jump. Press Space again in the air to jump higher and farther — the avatar does a random front flip, back flip, or spin. Drag to look around, scroll to zoom.
- The camera follows behind your avatar and swings round almost at once to face the way it's running, jumping, or turned. A held direction keeps going straight while the camera catches up. Dragging looks around; after you drag while standing still, the view stays put until you move again, and then it swings straight back behind you.
- Touch: the stick on the left moves you, Jump is on the right, and tapping Jump again in the air does the second jump. Dragging the course looks around.
- The course is generated as you go, with a new layout every day (UTC). Everyone gets the same layout that day, whatever level they start from or difficulty they pick, and the moving pads, sweeping bars, spiky gummies, and lava balls run on the wall clock so they line up for everyone.
- Play online (on by default, toggled on the start menu) puts you in the day's room with everyone else playing. Other players show up as their own avatars with a name tag, running, flipping, and doing their level-up dance, when they are on the part of the course near you. You don't get a name tag over your own avatar, so it never blocks your view. Players sitting in a menu are counted but not shown. The HUD shows how many players are online, you included. You can walk through other players; they don't block or push you.
  - It is peer-to-peer over WebRTC through [Trystero](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero): players find each other through public Nostr relays and then send poses straight to each other, so there is no game server. Other players can see your IP address, and a few strict networks can't connect without a TURN server.
  - Chat: press Enter (or the 💬 button) to type, Enter again to send, Escape to close. Lines go to everyone in the room, pop up in a bubble over the speaker for a few seconds, and fade from the log after a while. Typing never moves your avatar. A small built-in filter masks common swears and slurs in chat and names with asterisks, and you can only send a few lines in a row.
  - Friends: the 👥 button lists the players here. Tap Add friend to save them; they get a "… added you as a friend!" message, and don't need to add you back. Friends are saved in this browser with a little photo of their avatar, and show on the start menu with whether they're online right now (in today's room). The × removes a friend. Your friend id is a random code saved in this browser, so a friend added on one browser won't follow you to another.
  - Bots: when no one else is playing (or online play is off), Add a bot (start menu or 👥 panel) brings in a pretend player with a random name and look. It hops pad to pad a few pads around you, sometimes does a flip, dances on checkpoints, says friendly things, and now and then answers when you chat. Only you can see it. Remove bot sends it away.
  - To play with people elsewhere, the game has to be hosted somewhere public (for example GitHub Pages, Netlify, or Vercel). `npm run dev` is only reachable on your own network. Every 8th pad is a gold checkpoint dumpling with the level number on its flag. Touching it starts the next level and becomes your respawn point.
- Every run starts at level 1, on a pad just above the sea of the backdrop you picked. The course climbs about 7 meters per level. The sea or lava, the city, and the sky stay where they are, so they sink away below you as you climb. They seem to drop more slowly the higher you go, so the city stays in view far below.
- Higher up, the sea is too far down to land in. Falling well below the pads near you sends you back instead.
- From level 45 the climb goes into the clouds. They thicken as you go higher. By level 100 you're mostly in the clouds, with the city still showing through the gaps below. Past level 120 the sky darkens toward space.
- Capybara Prison Escape (levels 50 to 100): touching the level 50 checkpoint (jumpsuit orange, flashing alarm, CAPYBARA PRISON sign) fades the screen and you wake up in a prison cell, a building of its own beside the course. The course has no pads between level 50 and level 100.
  - The cell: climb the stool, the desk, and the top bunk to the vent. Every vent has a metal grate. Stand next to it and press E (or tap Open on touch screens) to swing it open.
  - The vent zig-zags left and right over the hallway, then you drop out of a hole in the hallway ceiling.
  - Police capybaras come out of their door and chase you up four flights of stairs. Barred cells with capybara inmates, some bouncing and some pacing, fill the middle of the stairwell. If the police catch you, the camera cuts to the capybara's face for a second and you're back in your cell.
  - Level 60: at the top of the stairs is the LAVA ROOM. The police can't come in: they wait at the door, stomping. A big red button by the lava has a gold padlock on it, and the key is hidden in one of six cupboards (a different one each time). Stand at a cupboard and press E (tap Open) to look inside; the key shows in the HUD once you have it. Without the key, the lock just says you need one. With it, press E at the lock (tap Unlock): the padlock pops off, and fifteen little metal cages (bars on three sides, a solid top to stand on) form on the walls one by one with a clank. Before that there's nothing there at all. They climb like stairs over the lava in three flights up a tall part of the room, with real gaps between them: along the back wall, back the other way along the wall facing it, then along the back wall again way up high. Jump from top to top, and the last one takes you up onto a top floor high up at the far end, about 11 meters above the lower floor. The door out is down on the lower floor at the bottom of a tall shaft, so take the ELEVATOR: step into the cage car at the edge of the top floor and press E (tap Ride). Its gate slides shut, it carries you smoothly all the way down for about 10 seconds (you can't move or fall out on the way, and the camera follows you down the shaft), dings at the bottom, and its other gate slides open toward the door. A barrier closes the top floor's edge whenever the car isn't up there. If the car is on the other floor, press E at the red call panel on the wall (tap Call) to bring it to you; with nobody in it, it also goes back up by itself a few seconds after reaching the bottom. You can ride it back up too. Falling in the lava (or touching it) sends you back to level 60, but you keep the key and the cages stay. Getting caught back to an earlier checkpoint (or starting over at level 50) puts the key back in a cupboard and the cages away. Walking back out the way you came gets you caught by the police.
  - Through the door on the lower floor, big squishies stuck to the walls climb round the room to a second vent, with real gaps and steps like the obby. Once you're in, the police slam open the metal side door in the room's west wall with a bang and come out of it after you, but they can't catch you on the squishies. The side door shuts again whenever you're sent back. If they catch you on the floor, you go back to level 60.
  - The second vent zig-zags left and right too, then comes out on a high ledge in the killer capybara's room. A very long zipline zig-zags back and forth high over the barred cells (you can see the inmates and the bots being chased below) for about 28 seconds, down to the level 75 checkpoint: walk under the handle, or press E (tap Grab on touch screens), and you hang on and slide all the way down. You can't steer on the way, and nothing can catch you.
  - Level 75: a killer capybara (glowing red eyes, fangs, spiked collar) chases you until you jump onto the obby over the green goo. If it catches you, you get the same close-up and go back to the level 75 checkpoint on the floor. Falling in the goo also sends you back.
  - Level 80: past the obby, a door off the far ledge leads to a room with a glowing vending machine. Press E (tap Buy) for an energy soda: it costs 3 coins, or it's free if you don't have 3. The soda makes you run and move in the air 2.6 times faster until you reach level 90 or get caught, with a glowing badge in the HUD. The kitchen door stays shut until you drink it. Then a chef capybara (tall hat, apron, cleaver, red eyes) bursts out, about 2.3 times faster than a normal run, and chases you for a minute or more: up the kitchen over or round the counters, across the pantry, down the freezer, and through the dining hall to the arena's side door. If it catches you, you get the close-up, go back to level 80, the soda wears off, and you can buy another.
  - Level 90: the giant capybara warden throws mandarins at you. You get a blaster: face the warden and hold F or click (or hold Shoot on touch screens). You have 10 lives, and 5 hits win. Lose all 10 and the fight starts over. Crates give you cover.
  - Beat the warden and the gate behind it opens. Run through it to come out on the level 100 checkpoint (FREEDOM! sign, 25 bonus coins the first time) and keep climbing the obby.
  - Bots in the prison do the whole escape with you: the vents, the stairs, the lava room (they open the right cupboard and unlock the lock too, which forms the cages for a few seconds while they climb across, then they ride the elevator down; it won't come down on you, and it never leaves while you're standing in it), the squishies, the zipline, and the chases. Your friends in the prison are all in the same place, so you see each other there.
  - You're the prisoner the whole way: your outfit switches to prison stripes (and any costume comes off) until you escape. Your saved look doesn't change. Pick Police under "Play as" on the main menu (or turn on "Play as police" in the Character studio) to wear a navy police uniform and cap instead, in the prison and out of it.
  - Playing as police, you catch prisoners. At level 50 you start in the hall instead of a locked cell, and prisoners break out one at a time and run up the police stairs. They're a little slower than you, so touch one to catch it: you get 10 coins, and the "Caught" counter in the top bar goes up. If it reaches the lava room door, it gets away. Online, touching another player who's playing as a prisoner in the prison catches them too, and so does touching a prisoner bot. They go back to their checkpoint. The police capybaras are on your side and don't chase you. From the lava room on, the escape is the same as for prisoners.
  - The police, the killer, and the chef are slower on Easy and faster on Hard, and so are the warden's throws.
  - The HUD shows your escape progress (0/50 to 49/50). Closing the game in the prison resumes at level 50, 60, 75, 80, or 90.
  - Once you've escaped, the Character studio has a "Play the prison escape again" button. It puts you back in the cell, and when you get out you go back to your own checkpoint. Replays never lower your best level or take away coins or unlocks.
- Lava or a hazard counts as a fall and puts you back on the last safe platform you stood on. A glowing arrow on its floor points to the next squishy, and a "Keep going!" message pops up.
- Green dumplings are springs. They launch you a big step up to the next squishy.
- Difficulty ramps up over the first 10 levels, then stays at its hardest:
  - Gaps get wider, steps get taller, pads shift more side to side, and the skinny foods and smaller toy blocks show up more often.
  - From level 2, some pads slide sideways.
  - From level 3, some pads bob up and down, and candy-striped bars sweep across wide pads.
  - From level 4, fireballs leap up from below between pads. A glowing ring below warns you just before one launches.
  - From level 5, spiky gummies orbit some pads.
  - On Easy there are no sweeping bars, spiky gummies, or lava balls. Medium and Hard have all three.
- Every gap is checked against the real jump physics, so every pad can be reached.
- You always start on the highest level you've reached (saved in this browser).
- The HUD shows your level and coins. Every checkpoint you move on to pays 5 coins, including the prison's 60, 75, 80, and 90, and they pay again when you reach them again (a prison replay, or a climb from an earlier level). Going back to a checkpoint after a fall never pays.
- The gear opens the character studio.
  - Girl or boy: boys get a short haircut, and dress outfits become a shirt with matching shorts and suspenders.
  - Age: Kid, Teen, or Grown-up. Older characters are taller, with longer legs, torso, and arms and a smaller head. Your character's size doesn't change jumping or collisions.
  - Also pick a face, a hair color (swatches, a custom color picker, or rainbow), an outfit, a skin tone, and a bow. The default is the Classic look: big grin, blonde hair, plaid pinafore, white knee socks. The preview updates live, and the look is saved in this browser.
  - Emo starter look: a boy with a shaggy black fringe over his eyes, a grey Inkprint hoodie covered in blackletter text with a fur-trimmed hood worn up, and light jeans with a black belt. The hair style (Long, Short, or Shaggy fringe) can also be picked on its own.
  - Play as police: the toggle (or Police under "Play as" on the main menu) dresses you in a navy police shirt and trousers with a gold star badge, a black belt, and a navy cap with a black peak. The preview shows it, other players see it online, and it's saved in this browser. Turn it off and your own outfit and costume come straight back.
  - Level-up dance: Cheer, Floss, Twirl, Robot, Disco, Jelly wiggle, Dab, Wave hello, Chicken dance, Star jumps, Sprinkler, Bunny hop, or No dance. The preview plays the dance you pick. When you reach a new level, your avatar turns to the camera and does it for about 2.5 seconds, and you can't move until it finishes.
- High graphics uses bloom, glow, film grain, a vignette, and a 2048 shadow map. Low turns those effects off and uses a 1024 shadow map.

Add `?debug` to the URL for a stats overlay.

Babylon.js is pinned to 9.29.0 (`@babylonjs/core`). Physics is the Havok plugin that ships with Babylon. The landing crunch (`sounds/crunch.mp3`) plays positionally from the pad through Audio Engine V2. Butter pads play `sounds/butter.mp3` instead, and `sounds/walking.mp3` loops while you walk. `sounds/menu-music.mp3` loops while a menu is open, and the picked course song loops quietly while you play. Jazz (`sounds/jazz.mp3`) is free. Lounge jazz, game music, DJ mix, and Party are bought with coins in the rewards shop and picked under Course music in Customize avatar. The first key, click, or tap unlocks sound.
